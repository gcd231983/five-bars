When a tower gets busy, carriers decide whose data goes first. Most cheap plans are deprioritized: they slow down before the carrier’s own premium customers. Plans with premium (priority) data keep full speed during congestion, which matters in cities, at stadiums and on campuses.

What to know before you switch

  • Premium data often has a monthly limit (for example 50GB or 100GB). After that, the line is deprioritized like a standard plan.
  • If you rarely notice slowdowns today, you probably don’t need to pay extra for priority.
  • Premium plans often bundle more hotspot data, which can make them worth it for remote workers.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest plan with premium data?

In our latest check, Total Wireless Total MAX 5G, bring your phone has the lowest estimated 12-month cost at about $250 ($20 a month). Prices change often, so confirm at the carrier before you buy.

What is the difference between premium and standard data?

Premium (priority) data gets the same treatment as the carrier’s top postpaid customers when a tower is congested. Standard data can be slowed first. When the network isn’t busy, both run at the same speed.
