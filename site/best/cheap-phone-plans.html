What to know before you switch

  • Know your data use before you shop. On an iPhone, open Settings → Cellular; on Android, look under Settings → Network → Data usage. If you use under 5GB a month, a small-data plan can cost half as much as unlimited.
  • Prepaid carriers (often called MVNOs) run on Verizon, AT&T or T-Mobile towers, so coverage is the same network. The usual trade-off is that your data can slow down first when a tower is busy.
  • Budget for the regular price, not the intro price. Many deals last 3–6 months and require a new line or a number transfer.
  • Paying a year upfront can be the cheapest option per month, but you lose flexibility if the service doesn’t work well where you live.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest phone plan right now?

In our latest check, Good2Go Mobile Annual 1GB (pay $60 upfront) has the lowest estimated 12-month cost at about $60, paid upfront for the year. Prices change often, so confirm at the carrier before you buy.

Which carriers include taxes in the price?

Of the plans we track, US Mobile, Total Wireless, Cricket, Visible and Metro by T-Mobile include taxes and fees in the advertised price. Others add them at checkout, which typically adds a few dollars a month.

Are cheap prepaid plans as good as Verizon, AT&T or T-Mobile?

They use the same towers, so coverage is usually identical. The differences are data priority when a tower is congested, mostly online customer service, and fewer perks such as streaming bundles or in-store help. For most people the savings outweigh those trade-offs.

Do these prices include taxes and fees?

It depends on the carrier. Some include taxes and regulatory fees in the advertised price; others add them at checkout. Each listing below says which.
