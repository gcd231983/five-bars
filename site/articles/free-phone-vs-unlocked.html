Is a $0 phone worth switching plans for?

A $20 monthly plan upgrade adds $480 over two years. Here’s when buying the same phone outright costs less.

By Gian De Cecco, editor of Five Bars · November 25, 2026

Get the free Excel workbook

The 24-month receipt

Move from $25 to $45 a month and you’ll spend an extra $480 on service. If the same phone costs $400 unlocked, I’d keep the cheaper plan and buy it outright. If my current phone still works, I’d keep it.

Illustrative example. One line, unchanged plan prices, the same new handset, no trade-in, and all credits earned. Taxes and fees are excluded. This isn’t a current carrier offer.

What changes my answer?

At $480 unlocked, the two upgrades tie. At $600 unlocked, the carrier offer saves $120 if every credit posts. The $45 plan could be worth more if it replaces something you already pay for, like separate hotspot service. Check that the network works where you need it before you switch.

Before you sign, check three numbers

  1. What you pay today. Include the first service charge, device taxes, down payment, and activation fees.
  2. How long credits last. Ask when the first credit posts and how long you must keep the qualifying plan. If a trade-in is required, compare the value of keeping or selling it yourself.
  3. What you owe if you leave. Ask what happens to your device balance and credits if you cancel or move to a cheaper plan.

What phone decision are you stuck on?

A model, two options, and rough prices are enough. Please don’t send a bill, account number, PIN, or device serial number.
