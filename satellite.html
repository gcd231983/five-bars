What’s still coming

AT&T: AT&T and AST SpaceMobile completed a handoff between AT&T’s towers and AST’s satellites on October 21, 2026. AT&T says it’s working toward a beta and then commercial service. There’s no public sign-up yet.

Verizon: Verizon signed a commercial agreement with AST SpaceMobile to offer satellite service “when needed” in the continental U.S., announced for 2026. We couldn’t confirm a public launch as of November 3, 2026.

AST’s service is designed for calls, texts and data on regular phones. Until it launches, regular phone calls by satellite aren’t available to U.S. consumers; T-Satellite supports voice chat only through WhatsApp.

Common questions

Do I need a special phone or antenna?
No antenna. You need a phone that supports it: iPhone 14 or newer and Pixel 9 or 10 have satellite features built in, and T-Mobile says T-Satellite works on most phones from the last four years. T-Mobile publishes a list of supported phones.
Can I make a normal phone call by satellite?
Not yet. T-Satellite supports voice chat through WhatsApp, and 911 by satellite is text only. AT&T and Verizon’s satellite partner has tested calls, but that service isn’t open to the public.
Does it work indoors or in a car?
It’s built for outdoors with a clear view of the sky. Trees, buildings and car roofs can block it, and messages can take a minute or more to go through.
Should I rely on it in an emergency?
It can help, but it isn’t guaranteed. T-Mobile notes that satellite 911 texts can be delayed and may go to a national response center instead of local 911. If you go deep into the backcountry, a dedicated satellite messenger is still the safer plan.
Will Apple start charging?
Apple hasn’t said. It has extended free access every year so far, most recently in September 2026 for iPhone 14, 15 and 16 owners.
